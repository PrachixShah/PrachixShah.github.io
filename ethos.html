Beliefs *

Working theories about how to live. Revised often, defended in person.

These change. I'm trying to keep the page honest about which ones I've actually tested and which ones I just like the sound of. The starred ones are the ones I'll go to the mat for.

On paying attention

* How much good is in a day depends partly on how much of it I actually catch. Looking straight at the hard parts and still noticing the friend who stayed, the stupid joke, the sky — that isn't denial. It's refusing to rush past my own life.

The reason to stargaze has nothing to do with astronomy. Something that big makes the self feel smaller, and for a while I take up less room in my own head. I'll drive hours past the light pollution for it.

On giving people room

* Give people slack. I'm quick to explain someone by their character and slow to remember their circumstances — a generous read is mostly me trying to supply the context I can't see. It should stay open to evidence.

Understanding someone isn't the same as signing up for a relationship with them. I can get exactly why things are the way they are and still decide it isn't what I want.

Leaving doesn't require a verdict that the other person is bad. Sometimes the regard just isn't mutual anymore, and that's reason enough.

On measuring things

* How you choose to measure something is a quiet admission of what you're willing to be honest about. Half of what I do is turning we can't really know that into a number someone will act on.

An estimate nobody checks is a feeling in a lab coat.

On honesty

I want to tell the truth whenever I get the chance. But saying what I believe and doing the work to find out whether I should believe it are two different jobs, and I need both.

Being honest about how I feel doesn't make my read of someone else's motives correct.

On arguing well

* Some of my favourite nights were philosophy arguments in Ottawa bars. Disagreement can be a kind of hospitality — someone trusts you enough to hand over an unfinished thought and watch what you do with it.

A belief I never let anyone push on quietly goes stale. Hearing the other side isn't about everyone being equally right; it's that mine is probably partial.

On keeping love around

* Build a life with love in more than one corner of it. Friends count as flourishing, not decoration.

When something's hard, go help someone else who's having a hard time. Buy them dinner, talk. The help doesn't have to fix anything.

Take two days off to see someone you love. Then take a third.
