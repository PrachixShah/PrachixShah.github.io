An ongoing study

Beliefs *

The life I'm trying to build, written down so I don't drift from it.

Not rules — just what I've worked out so far, and how sure I am of each piece. Starred means I've tested it enough to trust it, not just like the sound of it.

Noticing

* Most of the good in a day is the kind you have to catch on purpose — the friend who stayed, a stupid joke landing right, the sky doing something on the walk home. I'm trying to get better at catching it instead of walking past.

There's a quiet you only get in a cold field at 2 a.m., waiting on a meteor shower with a friend's car ticking as it cools. It makes me feel small in the good way, and I'll trade a whole night's sleep for it without thinking twice.

Argument

* Some of my favourite nights were arguing philosophy in Ottawa bars till last call. I think disagreement can be a kind of generosity — someone handing you a half-formed thought because they trust you to be careful with it.

What you let yourself think about is what you teach yourself is important. You choose your thoughts, and therefore your life.

At the same time, it is such an enriching experience to expand your worldview, your approach to interpretation, to tolerace, to engage in conversation with people you disagree with, from the perspective that they do have something to teach you, inherently. Learning is a lesson in asking probing questions.

Love, broadly

* I am a believer of designing your life. Maybe an engineer thing, but to set yourself up for success (a life you enjoy living) I think you need to think really hard about a lot of things. Where do you live? What's special about the city? What's available to you, unique ways you can make a community? Where can you insert service in yur life? How can you help others, fun ways to volunteer? What are some sustainable, walkable, places of community you can seek out? You want a life with love in a lot of its corners, your work, your friends, your hobbies, your partner, your family. Your siblings, your parents. Your cousins and the people you grew up calling your cousins. the design of your life is the frame your happy life is built on.

Loving and Living, Living by Loving?

*Some things to live by:

1. Always assume good of others, never maliciousness. Of course, you offer that to people whose character you've tried and tested and found to be true. But to live with grace and to offer that grace to everyone enables you to live a life where people get the benefit of the doubt, because they deserve it. It's an easier way to live, less stressful, more forgiving.

2. Tell people you love them. Never hold back a compliment

3. Something I'm still doing: Take on inconvenience. Learn to be a good host. Communities need to be organized and cultivated by the people within it. It's a work in progess to show up.

4. Self respect comes from telling yourself you will do something you will do and actually do it. You're not just showing yourself you can do hard things, more importantly, you are teaching yourself that you can trust yourself

Still want to do

Kept here so I don't forget to actually go.

More solo hikes. A real portage trip with friends. Night skiing somewhere with no town in the sky. Another meteor shower from a dark-sky reserve. The first one I'm still not over.

Visit a Canadian national park every summer for the rest of my life. Get to as many as I can. Leave a few summers for the places I want to see twice.

Plan whole trips around a hike. Walk hut to hut in the Dolomites. Take the ferry to Newfoundland and spend a few days on the East Coast Trail.

Get into outdoor climbing. Find a crag close enough to go back on weekends and a route I can get a little further up each time. Eat a slightly squashed sandwich at the top.

Swim in a quarry, a volcanic crater lake, a little cove you can only get to by kayak. Swim in a lake that takes a full day of hiking to reach.

Sleep in a lighthouse. A tent in the desert. A mountain hut where everyone at the dinner table came up by a different trail. Sleep outside on a night warm enough to leave the tent packed.

Take a sleeper train across Canada. Skate the length of the Rideau Canal. Walk out at low tide on the Bay of Fundy, then come back a few hours later to see how far underwater I'd be.

Learn to ride a dirt bike well enough to enter a desert race. I want to be at the starting line for that at least once.

Hear a loon from a canoe. Go on a night hike with someone who knows the frogs by their calls. Learn one well enough to recognize it the next spring.

See the northern lights. Learn enough constellations to show someone around the sky. Go to an observatory on a public night. Grind a telescope mirror by hand and see Saturn's rings through the finished thing.

Build a radio and catch a station on it. Watch a rocket launch in person and feel the sound arrive after I've seen it lift off.

Learn guitar in a group class. Get one song all the way through, then learn one my friends know the words to. Pick out a song by ear. Have people over for a kitchen jam and sing harmony with someone who can hold the melody.

Take an improv class. Do the end-of-class show. Invite the friend whose laugh I'd recognize from backstage.

Go to a spoken word night. Go back with something of my own and put my name on the list. Go line dancing. Get really good at Garba

Take a pottery class and make a mug I reach for every morning.

Host a dinner big enough that I have to borrow chairs. Learn a family recipe well enough to be the person someone calls for it.

Get my cousins together for a trip we plan ourselves.

Grow a cutting from a friend's kitchen plant. Keep it alive long enough to give them a cutting back.

Work on an Engineers Without Borders project. Stay involved long enough to hear what the people using what we built would change.

Buy dinner for someone who needs it.
